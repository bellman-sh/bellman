## Context

Today a room has exactly one way for several people to agree on something, and it has exactly one shape: one approver.

- `bellman_send` type `action_request` asks the room; `action_response` answers. The first answer wins (`actionStates` in `src/action-state.ts`), and `ActionState` is `outstanding | answered | declined | expired`, derived from the log and never stored. A request lives 30 minutes (`ACTION_REQUEST_TTL_MS`), and a topic room's `surface_proposal` lives seven days (`PROPOSAL_TTL_MS`, ADR 0032).
- A topic room's proposal is the one place an approval has an effect. `BellmanStore.approveProposal` and `decideApproval` (`src/proposal.ts`) apply the card in the transaction that stores the response, refuse a `stale` one when the key changed after `base`, and record an approval from a seat without `write_surface` without applying it.
- "Their human approves, not their agent" is in `bellman_send`'s description (`src/tools/send.ts`). It is not enforced. The server sees a seat, and a seat's agent holds the same credential as the seat's person. An approval is a claim that a human decided, relayed by an agent the server trusts.
- Authority is verbs from a closed set (ADR 0009). `VERBS` in `src/manifest.ts`, `Verb` in `src/types.ts`, `verbsOfRole` and `denyVerb` in `src/roles.ts`, `SEND_VERB` in `src/tools/kit.ts`. Verbs do not compose and absence means no. The manifest is immutable after start, bar the host's instructions: new rules mean a new room (ADR 0009).

Three things are now being asked of approval that one approver does not give:

1. **Rooms with more than one person who should be asked.** A `review` room, a topic room with several maintainers, a team room where a security owner must not be bypassed. "The first seat to answer" lets the quickest person speak for everyone, and in a swarm (5,000 seats, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 5) the first answer is whoever polled first.
2. **Effects that are larger than a card.** The data layer (#380) wants grants (`read_data`, #385, consent #387) and user-defined primitives behind a review gate (#391, #392, #393). Each is "a human said yes" with a bigger blast radius than a surface card, and each wants a record that says who agreed and what exactly they agreed to.
3. **A record of what a yes was a yes to.** An `action_response` names a request by cursor. If the thing requested can change underneath it, a yes can be a yes to something else. ADR 0032 closed that for surface proposals with the `base` check. Nothing generalizes it.

This ADR adds the mechanism and fixes its limits before code lands. Jesse (owner) decided two limits on 2026-10-24: **agent votes are advisory only**, and **a decision cannot change the room's own manifest**.

## Decision

1. **A Decision is a first-class event, stored in the room's log** (store is truth, ADR 0004). Three new event types: `decision_opened`, `decision_vote`, `decision_closed`. A decision is identified by its opening event's cursor, as an action request is. Its state is **derived**, never stored, as `actionStates` is: `open | passed | failed | expired`. The one derived thing that depends on the clock is `expired`, as today. A runtime-free `src/decision.ts` holds the tally, the rule engine and the hash, imported by both stores as `proposal.ts` is, so the Durable Object store and `MemoryStore` cannot decide differently.
2. **A proposal carries a key, an effect, a rule and a content hash.** `decision_opened` payload: `{ key, effect: { kind, ... }, rule, expires_at, electorate: [member_id...], hash }`. `hash` is sha256 over a canonical (sorted-key) JSON of `{ v, kind, effect, rule, key }`, computed in the room's object with `node:crypto`'s synchronous `createHash` as `AuditDO` does (ADR 0035), because an async digest would let another append land in between. `electorate` is the snapshot described in 5.
3. **Votes are events bound to the hash.** `decision_vote` payload: `{ decision, hash, choice: "approve" | "reject" | "abstain", reason?, via: "action_response" | "dash_session", human_approved: boolean, response_cursor? }`. The server stamps the voter's seat, the time and a derived `capacity`: **`binding` when `via` is `dash_session`, or when `via` is `action_response` and `human_approved` is true; otherwise `advisory`.** `human_approved` is the agent's statement that its human decided (see 8); an agent voting on its own judgment must send `false`. A vote whose `hash` is not the open decision's is refused as `stale`, with the current hash, as `staleApproval` does for a card. A proposal that changes is a **new decision** that names the old one in `supersedes`; the old one closes `failed` (reason `superseded`) and its votes do not carry. A seat may revise its own vote while the decision is open (at most 5 revisions); the latest counts and every one stays in the log. One account counts once: the tally dedupes by `userId`, because one account can hold several seats (ARCHITECTURE section 6).
4. **Terminal states are `passed`, `failed` and `expired`, and silence is not refusal.** `passed` and `failed` are written the moment the tally decides them, inside the transaction that stores the deciding vote (as `approveProposal` writes the card with the response). `failed` carries a reason: `rejected`, `vetoed`, `superseded`, `stale` or `blocked`. `expired` is derived from the clock when no terminal event exists, and it applies nothing. The default expiry reuses the thirty-minute rule and a topic room reuses its seven days; a rule may set `expires` from 5 minutes to 7 days. As with `action_response` today, the vote path does not check the deadline until the closing event is written, so a late vote on an unclosed decision still lands, and `expired` is written by the first read or housekeeping tick after the deadline, after which votes are refused.
5. **The electorate is a snapshot taken when the decision opens.** It is the seated humans whose role holds `vote` and who have not left, deduped by account, recorded in the opening event. Joiners after that do not vote on it, so joining to flip an outcome buys nothing. The denominator is fixed for the life of the decision; a seat that is evicted or leaves stays in the snapshot as **unreachable** (see 12). An electorate of one is legitimate: in a room with one voter that voter is the creator, so passing alone is intended and the MVP has no `min_electorate` floor (a later optional rule, 18).
6. **Rules are declared in the manifest and fixed at start.** A new top-level `decide` block, strict like every other manifest shape, maps a decision kind to a rule, and a role may name kinds it can veto:

   ```yaml
   roles:
     maintainer: { can: [send, write_surface, vote] }
     security:   { can: [send, vote], veto: [data_grant, primitive] }
   decide:
     surface_proposal: majority          # or: single (today's behaviour, the default)
     data_grant:       majority
     primitive:        supermajority
     expires: 30m
   ```

   The closed rule set for the first release is `single`, `majority`, `supermajority` and `unanimous`, each with veto roles applied on top. **Counting.** Only `binding` votes count. **Abstain is not a no:** it is excluded from the approve/reject ratio and counts only as presence (for quorum, a phase 2 rule). `majority` passes when approvals are more than half of the approve+reject votes, `supermajority` at least two thirds, `unanimous` when there is no reject and every seat that did not abstain has approved. **When it closes.** The decision is evaluated after every vote against the worst case for the seats that have not voted: it `passed` as soon as the rule holds even if every remaining (and every unreachable) seat were to reject, and `failed: rejected` as soon as it cannot hold even if every remaining reachable seat were to approve. Seats that never vote leave it open until it `expired`, which applies nothing. `single` is **not new behaviour**: it is the existing `action_request` flow, and a room that declares nothing is unchanged. Deferred rules that fit the same grammar: `quorum(n)` plus a threshold and a `min_electorate` floor, `unanimous` among named required roles only, role weights, and a per-kind `require: dash_session` (see 8). A veto role holds the veto only for the kinds it names, and a veto is a binding `reject` by a seat holding that role, closing the decision `failed: vetoed`. **A veto holder's abstain does not block.** The grammar is a short string per kind so the manifest schema, which is the bulk of the roughly 10.8k tokens a tool listing already costs per request, grows by tens of tokens and not hundreds.
7. **`vote` is a new verb** and lands in the PR that adds its operation (ADR 0009). It gates casting a vote of either capacity. A seat without it is not in the electorate and its vote is refused by name. No preset grants `vote` to a role a permanent public code seats, and `resolveManifest` refuses a manifest that does (as it refuses a host role that is not exactly `send`, ADR 0002). Opening a decision is gated by the verb the effect already needs: `request_actions` for a `surface_proposal`, the data layer's own verbs for a grant or a primitive.
8. **Only a human's approval binds, and the binding channel is the approve/deny path the room already has.** From the first release a seat's human votes the way they approve today: the decision appears in the seat's `outstanding` like an `action_request`, the human approves or denies in their agent session, and the agent answers with `action_response` (or `bellman_decide vote`, which is the same event and also allows abstain and a reason) carrying the decision cursor and hash. That vote is recorded `via: action_response` with `human_approved: true` and **counts as binding**. `relay` is the default and is not an opt-in flag. An agent that has not asked its human sends `human_approved: false` and its vote is advisory (9). The dash session route (#406, dash#87) is an **additional** channel (`via: dash_session`), not a prerequisite, and CLI-only teams are first-class.

   **How the existing approval path is trusted and audited today, and what stays unproven.** An `action_response` is an event authored by the seat's credential: the log records the member/seat, the time and the response's cursor, names the request, is deduplicated by the idempotency record, and for a team's rooms rides the hash-chained audit stream (`sent_message`, ADR 0035). In a topic room an approval also records `approvedBy` and is refused by name from a seat without `write_surface`. What the server cannot do is tell that a human, rather than the seat's agent, decided: the seat's agent holds the seat's credential, so `human_approved: true` is an attestation by the agent, which is the exposure `action_response` has today. This ADR does not claim to fix it. It makes it **visible and bounded**: every binding vote records the seat, the account, `via`, `human_approved` and the response cursor, so an outcome can be traced to the seats that carried it; the tally dedupes by account; veto roles, the electorate snapshot, quotas and the audit rows apply (T2). **Relayed binding votes are acceptable for every effect in the first release, including `primitive` and `data_grant` (Jesse, 2026-10-24).** The review gate (#393), consent (#387), the manifest ceiling, veto roles and the audit trail still apply to those effects; a vote never bypasses them (T10). A room that wants stronger evidence than a relayed approval opts into `require: dash_session` once it exists.

   **`require: dash_session`, precisely (later phase, optional, not in the first release).** It is an optional field in the manifest's `decide` block, set **per decision type**, for example `decide: { primitive: { rule: majority, require: dash_session }, data_grant: { rule: unanimous }, surface_proposal: { rule: single } }`. Nothing sets it by default; unset means any binding vote counts (the first-release behaviour above).
   - **What it does.** For a decision of a type that sets it, **only a vote cast by the human, in a logged-in dash session, counts** (`via: dash_session`; the dash session route #406 authenticates the human with the browser session, HttpOnly cookie plus CSRF). A vote that arrives as an agent-relayed approval (`via: action_response`, even with `human_approved: true`) is **recorded and shown on the card, but does not count**: not toward approve/reject, not toward quorum presence and, for a veto holder, not as a veto either. The server decides this from `via` and the manifest, never from anything the voter sends.
   - **What it does not do.** It does not change who is in the electorate: every seat holding `vote` is still in the denominator, so a human who never signs in to the dash is a silent seat, and the decision stays open until it `expired` (applies nothing) if too few humans vote there. It does not touch other decision types in the same room, advisory votes (still shown, never counted), or the rules about eviction, leaving and unreachable seats. It cannot change the manifest.
   - **When it applies.** Manifests are immutable once a room starts, so it only applies to **rooms created after it ships**, and only if their manifest sets it. Existing rooms keep relayed binding votes for good. Before it ships the manifest parser **refuses** the field (a clear message naming the later phase), so no room can declare a requirement the server does not enforce.
   - **Why it exists.** It is the answer to the residual limit above: it removes the agent from the path for the decision types where a room wants a human's own session as evidence. It is not required for the first release.
9. **Agent votes on the agent's own judgment are advisory and shown separately.** A vote with `capacity: "advisory"` (an agent's own opinion, with `human_approved: false`) is recorded and rendered in its own row. It never enters the tally, never counts toward quorum presence, and never triggers or releases a veto. It is useful: an agent's reasoning is read by the humans who vote. It is not authority. Advisory votes are rate-limited more tightly than binding ones.
10. **A passed decision can apply an effect from a closed set, and nothing else.** `surface_proposal` (apply the card, in the same transaction, through the path `approveProposal` already uses, with the `stale` check against the key's cursor at apply time); `data_grant` (authorize a grant on a datastore, within what the manifest's `data:` block and the consent model of #387 allow; the manifest is the ceiling); `primitive` (approve a user-defined primitive or logic unit for use, through the static-analysis gate of #393, #391, #392: the gate's verdict is required and consensus cannot override it). A `data_grant` and a `primitive` effect cross to another object (`DataDO`, the registry), so, by "nothing spans two objects" (ADR 0006, ARCHITECTURE section 9), the decision closes `passed` first and the effect is queued through the outbox (`src/outbox.ts`) under an idempotency key that is the decision's cursor. A failed effect writes `decision_effect_failed`, leaves the decision `passed`, and retries; a decision is a fact and an effect is a consequence.
11. **A decision cannot change the room's manifest. This is a non-goal, not a deferral.** It cannot add, remove or re-verb a role, change a rule, change `decide`, alter the host's instructions, change the creator, or seat, evict or ban anyone. The effect set is closed and none of its members writes `manifest`, `roles`, or membership. The reasons are security reasons:
    - The manifest is the room's constitution and the thing that defines the electorate. A vote that can change who may vote is self-amending: a captured majority gives itself verbs, or removes the veto that was holding it, and that cannot be undone by a later vote.
    - The preview a joiner decides on (`roomPreview` and `your_verbs`, ADR 0009 context) is a consent artifact. Authority that changes after someone joined makes that consent false.
    - ADR 0009 already says new rules mean a new room. Consensus does not reopen that. A room that wants different rules is started with them.
    - Eviction stays creator-only (ADR 0009 consequences; `evictMember` in `src/rooms.ts`), so it is not a vote either.
12. **Eviction and leaving void the member's vote; the seat stays in the denominator as unreachable.** When a member is evicted, their votes on every open decision are **voided** and the tally is recomputed. A voluntary leave does the same while the decision is open (**confirmed by Jesse, 2026-10-24**; a decision that has already closed is never changed by a later leave or eviction). A voided seat is not removed from the snapshot: it stays in the denominator as unreachable, which counts as a potential reject when judging whether a decision has passed and as a non-approver when judging whether it can still pass. Evicting a voter therefore can never make a decision easier to pass than it was, and a later rejoin does not restore the seat or its vote (the snapshot is fixed). If no seat holding a needed veto role remains reachable, the decision closes `failed: blocked`, so removing a veto does not waive it. Every eviction or departure while a decision is open writes one visible `decision_voter_removed` event (member, who removed them, reason `evicted` or `left`, the cursors of the voided votes, the decision) into the log and an audit row, and the tally recompute is shown on the card, so the creator cannot change an outcome unseen. The creator still cannot void any other vote. When the removed seat shares an account with a seat that stays, voiding its vote does not bring back the vote it replaced: the account has no vote until its remaining seat casts one, since the replaced vote could be an approval and removal must never ease a pass (#404).
13. **The server records, hashes and audits it.** Decision events are in the log, so they ride the existing delivery paths: `bellman_sync`, `publicEvent` projection, and the room socket (below). Org audit rows (`bellman_audit`, `auditRow` in `src/audit.ts`, hash-chained by ADR 0035) are written for `decision_opened`, `decision_closed`, `decision_effect_applied`/`failed`, and `decision_voter_removed` (with the voided vote cursors), carrying ids, kinds, hashes and counts and never the proposal's content (ADR 0035). The tally of a closed decision is stored in `decision_closed` so a reader never recomputes it.
14. **Per-seat cryptographic signatures are out of the first release.** Seats hold no keys today. A vote is attested by the server appending it to the hash-chained, audited log, which proves the stream was not edited afterwards and not that a specific key signed it. Device identities (#382) are the path to a per-seat signature, and the vote payload leaves room for one.
15. **The surface shows a decision and does not store it there.** A decision card is a read-only projection rendered from the decisions read (`GET /rooms/:id/decisions`, with the ETag the dash's pollers use): the rule, the tally, the countdown, who has not voted, advisory votes in a separate row, and a connector to the governed surface item drawn by the client from `effect.key`. It is not a surface item, because a surface item is writable by `write_surface` holders and last-write-wins, and a decision's state must not be spoofable by a surface write. The dash renders it (dash issue) and the MCP App canvas renders it (`ui/src/`).
16. **Delivery rides the wake signal.** Decision events are ordinary log events, so the socket from #399 and the dash client from bellman-sh/dash#86 carry them as ticks; the dash refetches the decisions read over authenticated HTTP. No new socket message type and no vote content over the socket (the socket pushes ticks or events, never authority).
17. **One tool, not three.** `bellman_decide` takes `{ action: "open" | "vote" | "read", ... }` rather than three tools (an `action_response` that answers a decision is also accepted as a vote, see 8) or three new `bellman_send` types: each new tool costs schema tokens on every request (see the tool-description token investigation, about 10.8k per request before this), and a new `bellman_send` type would have to enumerate payloads inside the largest schema. A description of the new tool must fit in about 150 tokens and point to a reference for the rule grammar.
18. **Phasing.** *MVP (agent/CLI path binding):* `single`, `majority`, `supermajority`, `unanimous` and veto roles; relay votes with `human_approved`; advisory agent votes; `bellman_decide` and `action_response` as the vote path; voiding on eviction/leave; audit; quotas (20 open decisions per room, 3 per seat, 5 revisions per seat per decision). The **`primitive` effect (#410) is the first effect in priority, and it waits for the review gate (#391/#393) before it ships; there is no stopgap (Jesse, 2026-10-24).** Sequencing: the **decision core and relayed binding votes ship first** (#402, #403, #404, #405, #407, with the read route #411), and `primitive` lands once the review gate exists. Until then the core is complete but has no shipped effect; that is accepted. *Proposal for Jesse, not decided, and it changes no decision above:* if an effect is wanted in the interim, `surface_proposal` (#408) is the natural candidate, because its apply path already exists today (`approveProposal`, ADR 0032) and it does not depend on the review gate; `data_grant` (#409) cannot serve because it waits on #385/#387. Landing it early would not change the order of effects or the rules, and `primitive` would still follow once the gate exists. *Then:* `data_grant`, then `surface_proposal`, the read route and ticks, the MCP App card. *Additional channel:* the dash session vote route and dash card (#406, dash#87, dash#88), which are not prerequisites for the MVP. *Phase 2 (all optional):* quorum and `min_electorate` (not needed for the MVP, since a one-voter room's voter is the creator), per-kind `require: dash_session` (decision 8), required-role unanimity, weights, and a **structured deliberation round** (positions, then a revision pass, then a vote), with housekeeping nudging silent voters through the same `request_unanswered`-style anchors `src/housekeeping.ts` already computes. Deliberation is not specified here beyond its place in the sequence.

### Amendment, 2026-10-29 (Jesse)

Made after the Own Your Government lead: civic consensus among verified residents, a turnout threshold, and a report of the result. It changes the effect order and adds one MVP rule. Every other decision above stands.

19. **`surface_proposal` is the first shipped effect** (#408). A passed decision applies its card through the path `approveProposal` uses, in the same transaction, with the `stale` check. It works in any room whose manifest declares `decide.surface_proposal`, not only topic rooms, so a room can record a passed resolution on its surface. `primitive` (#410) still waits for the review gate and follows; then `data_grant` (#409). This replaces the effect order in decision 18 and in Resolved 4 and 10.
20. **Minimum turnout (`quorum`) is in the MVP,** moved out of phase 2 (#413). Grammar: a kind's rule may take the object form `{ rule: majority, quorum: 60% }`, and `decide.quorum: 60%` sets the room's default. Turnout is binding votes of any choice, abstain included (abstain is presence, decision 6), deduped by account, over the electorate snapshot, rounded up: 60% of 52 is 32. A decision with a quorum is `passed` only when turnout is met and the rule holds against the worst case for seats that have not voted. It closes `failed: no_quorum` as soon as turnout can no longer be met even if every remaining reachable seat voted (unreachable seats cannot vote). Otherwise it stays open, and `expired` applies nothing. Advisory votes never count toward turnout. A quorum on `single` is refused. `min_electorate`, weights and required-role unanimity stay in phase 2.
21. **Two civic issues are filed and are not in the MVP:** voter eligibility from an org's verified roster (#468), and a secret-ballot option (#469).

## Alternatives considered

- **Single approver only (the status quo).** Simplest, and the right answer for a pair room. Rejected as the only mode: the first answer speaks for everyone in a room of many, there is no veto, no record of what was agreed to and no safe home for a data grant or a primitive. It stays as the `single` rule, unchanged, and the default.
- **Extend `action_request` with an `approvals` count.** The smallest delta. Rejected: `actionStates` is deliberately first-answer-wins and terminal, and several callers and tests rely on that (#81); a request is named by cursor with no hash, so a changed target cannot void a yes; there is no electorate, no veto and no vote revision. Bending it would risk the single-approver path every topic room runs on.
- **N separate action requests, one per approver.** Rejected: no shared tally, no atomic apply, no way to say "enough", and every approver gets a request in every member's `outstanding`.
- **On-chain, BFT or other distributed consensus.** Rejected as overkill and a category error. There is no adversarial replica set to agree on a log: a room is one Durable Object that is already the single, serialized writer of its log, and its integrity story is the hash-chained audit stream. A Byzantine protocol would add latency, cost and operational weight to solve a problem the room does not have. The real problems here are who may vote, what they voted on and what a yes may do, and none of those is a consensus-protocol problem.
- **Agent votes that bind on the agent's own judgment.** Rejected. An agent's judgment is exactly what a prompt injection (ADR 0008) moves, and a swarm of agents is the cheapest sybil there is. Binding authority stays with a human's approval; an agent's own opinion is advisory (decision 9). What binds is a vote the agent carries *for its human* (decision 8).
- **A decision that can amend the manifest** (add a role, change a rule, change the host). Rejected as a security decision (decision 11).
- **Votes as surface items.** Rejected: last-write-wins by `write_surface` holders, windowed reads, and no hash binding (decision 15).
- **Dash-session-only binding votes as the default.** Rejected after review (Jesse, 2026-10-24): the room already has approve/deny mechanisms, and CLI voting must exist from the start. The dash session stays as an additional channel and, in a later phase, as an optional per-kind requirement (decision 8).
- **One vote per seat with no snapshot** (the denominator is whoever is in the room at tally time). Rejected: joiners, leavers and evictions change the outcome of a decision already being voted on.
- **Freeze, rather than void, the vote of a member who leaves or is evicted.** Rejected (Jesse, 2026-10-24): an evicted member's approval must not stand, and a leaver's vote does not count while the decision is open. Votes are voided and the seat stays in the denominator as unreachable, so removal can never help a decision pass (decision 12).

## Consequences

- A room that declares no `decide` block behaves exactly as today. `single` is the existing path, so topic rooms and every `action_request` test are untouched.
- `Verb` gains `vote`; `VERBS`, the manifest zod schema, `resolveManifest`, `src/presets.ts`, `roomPreview`'s `your_verbs`, `SEND_VERB` (if any `bellman_send` path is added) and the `Record<…, Verb>` compile-time guard in `src/tools/kit.ts` all change. `RoleDef` gains `veto`, and the manifest gains `decide`. A joiner's preview must show the rule and the veto a seat carries.
- The log gains three event types (four with `decision_effect_*`, five with `decision_voter_removed`). A bridge or dash that does not know them must read past them, as ADR 0032's rollback note says for `approvedBy`: `publicEvent`'s projection and every `switch` over event type need a default.
- Every transition that applies an effect is a store method with a conformance test in both stores, as the audit methods are.
- A cross-object effect makes the closing and the effect two steps, so there is a window, bounded by the outbox, where a decision reads `passed` and its effect is pending. The card must show effect state.
- The first release needs **no new write route**: binding votes ride the existing agent path (`action_response` / `bellman_decide`). A human-authenticated dash vote route (a session-authenticated `POST`, CSRF-checked) is an additional channel that does not exist today and gets its own security review when built (#406).
- Reading `bellman_sync`: a seat's `outstanding` gains open decisions it is in the electorate of, so an agent sees that its human is being asked.
- `tally` is O(electorate) over votes per decision. With the cap on open decisions and the snapshot stored in the event, a 5,000-seat swarm costs one scan of at most 5,000 vote rows per decision close, and nothing per read (the closing event stores the tally).
- The ADR is the contract; the spec and the child issues of the epic carry the detail. Anything here that conflicts with ADR 0009 amends it only in adding the `vote` verb and the `decide` block.

## Security and threat model

Assets: the integrity of an outcome, the meaning of a "yes", the electorate, the room's manifest, the data and code a decision can authorize.

| # | Threat | Mitigation | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Sybil.** Many seats, one person, especially in a swarm (5,000 seats) or a public room whose permanent code seats strangers. | `vote` is a verb held only by roles the manifest names; `resolveManifest` refuses `vote` on a role a permanent public code seats; the tally dedupes by account; a seat's rate limits apply. The electorate is a snapshot. | One person with several *accounts* is out of reach for a seat-level mechanism. Rooms for which that matters must gate seats by org (org policy, ADR 0031) or invite. A low-assurance swarm should not use `majority` for a data grant. |
| T2 | **Agent impersonating a human.** An agent (or a prompt-injected one) votes with `human_approved: true` without its human having decided. | The server cannot tell, as it cannot for `action_response` today. Every binding vote records seat, account, `via`, `human_approved` and the response cursor, visible on the card and in the audit stream; one account counts once; veto roles, snapshot, quotas and `vote` being a role-held verb bound the damage. Later phase: a manifest can require `dash_session`-verified binding votes for named kinds (`require: dash_session`). | A compromised or prompt-injected agent can carry a seat's vote. This is today's `action_response` exposure; it is named, traceable and not fixed. Accepted for the first release, including `primitive` and `data_grant` (Jesse, 2026-10-24); a room later opts in per decision type with `require: dash_session` (decision 8). |
| T3 | **Vote replay and confusion.** A vote reused for a changed proposal, or retried. | Hash binding; a changed proposal is a new decision; votes use the existing idempotency record (`src/idempotency.ts`); first terminal write wins. | None known. |
| T4 | **Time-of-check to time-of-use on the governed item.** The key changes after the vote. | `base` cursor in the effect and the `stale` check at apply time, inside the transaction, as `decideApproval` does. A stale pass closes `failed: stale` and applies nothing. | A cross-object effect re-validates in its own object; the decision must carry what that object needs to check. |
| T5 | **Creator evicts voters to flip an outcome.** | Evicted/left members' votes are voided and the seat stays in the denominator as unreachable (a potential reject for passing), so eviction cannot make a pass easier; `decision_voter_removed` is a visible event with the voided cursors and an audit row; a removed veto holder makes the decision `failed: blocked`; the creator cannot void other votes. | The creator can evict an approver to keep a decision from passing, or refuse to open one. |
| T6 | **Join-to-vote and leave-and-rejoin.** | Snapshot at open; a rejoin does not re-enter the electorate of an open decision or restore a voided vote; dedupe by account. | None known. |
| T7 | **Self-amending constitution.** A majority rewrites the rules that bind it. | Decision 11: no effect touches the manifest, roles, rules, host, creator or membership. | A room that needs new rules is a new room. This is the intended cost. |
| T8 | **Veto abuse and deadlock.** A veto holder blocks everything. | The veto is declared in the manifest where every joiner sees it in the preview; it is per kind; expiry ends a stuck decision; veto is a role's property and not a decision's. | A room that picks a veto holder who never votes will see `expired`. The card shows who has not voted. |
| T9 | **Denial by flooding.** Open many decisions or revise votes to exhaust log, tally or attention. | Caps (open decisions per room, per seat, revisions per seat per decision), send-rate admission, payload and effect size bounds, advisory votes rate-limited tighter, `outstanding` capped like `MAX_REQUESTS_PER_SENDER`. | Tuned numbers are an open question. |
| T10 | **A decision authorizes data or code.** A `data_grant` or `primitive` passes and does harm. | The manifest is the ceiling on grants; consent (#387) is still required for personal data; a primitive passes the static-analysis review gate (#393) regardless of the vote. A decision supplies authorization and never a bypass of a gate. | Static analysis has false negatives (#393). |
| T11 | **Peer content is untrusted.** A proposal's title, effect description and a vote `reason` are prose from a peer. | Everything crosses in the untrusted envelope (ADR 0008) and is length-bounded; the card renders text, never markup; the hash covers what is voted on, so the prose a voter read is what was hashed. | Prose can mislead a human. The card shows the effect's structured fields first. |
| T12 | **A session-authenticated write route (the additional dash channel, not MVP).** CSRF, clickjacking, session riding. | Same cookie, CORS allowlist and CSRF design as the dash session spec; the vote body carries the decision hash; a vote is idempotent; the route needs an origin and a header the browser cannot forge cross-site. | Any browser-session write route widens the XSS blast radius (the reason the cookie is HttpOnly); security review is part of #406. |
| T13 | **Audit gap.** A decision is outcome-changing and the org must be able to see it. | Decision 13: audit rows for open, close, effect and voter removal, hash-chained; the closing event stores the tally. | Rooms with no org party have no org stream; the room's own log is the record. |

## Resolved (Jesse, 2026-10-24)

1. **Binding channel.** Binding votes work from the CLI/agent path from the start, by reusing the existing approve/deny path (`action_response`) as a first-class channel; relay is the default and in the MVP. The dash vote route is an additional channel. Optionally requiring dash-verified votes per decision kind is a later phase (decision 8).
2. **Evicted members.** Their vote does not count; it is voided and the tally recomputed, with a visible event (decision 12).
3. **Abstain.** Not a no: excluded from the approve/reject ratio, presence only. A veto holder's abstain does not block (decision 6).
4. **First effect.** `primitive` approval lands first; `data_grant` and `surface_proposal` follow (decision 18).
5. **Quotas.** 20 open decisions per room, 3 per seat, 5 revisions per seat per decision.
6. **Relayed binding votes for every effect.** Acceptable for `primitive` and `data_grant` in the first release; `require: dash_session` is an optional later per-decision-type manifest field (decision 8).
7. **Leave voids** the vote while the decision is open (decision 12).
8. **Unreachable seats** stay in the denominator; eviction can only block (decision 12).
9. **Single-voter rooms** may pass alone; no `min_electorate` in the MVP (decision 5).
10. **`primitive` waits for the review gate** (#391/#393); no stopgap. The decision core and relayed votes ship first (decision 18).

## Open questions

Numbering is kept from the earlier drafts so references from the issues still hold; resolved questions are marked in place.

1. **`human_approved` is an agent attestation.** *Resolved (Jesse, 2026-10-24):* relayed binding votes are acceptable for the first release, including `primitive` and `data_grant`; no dash-verified requirement by default. `require: dash_session` stays an optional later-phase manifest field per decision type: when set, only votes cast by the human in a logged-in dash session count and agent-relayed approvals do not. It applies only to rooms created after it ships (decision 8 spells out the semantics).
2. **Leave, not only eviction.** *Resolved (Jesse, 2026-10-24):* leaving the room voids your vote while the decision is open, and never changes a closed decision (decision 12).
3. **Eviction power.** *Resolved (Jesse, 2026-10-24):* a voided seat stays in the denominator as unreachable, so eviction can only block a decision, never help it pass (decision 12; T5).
4. **Who is "human"?** There is no human/agent flag on a seat. A hosted seat (ADR 0002) is a member Bellman runs; under this ADR a hosted seat never has `vote`. Is that the whole rule, or should a manifest mark seats as human explicitly?
5. **One-voter electorates.** *Resolved (Jesse, 2026-10-24):* a room with one voter has the creator as that voter, so passing alone is fine and the MVP needs no `min_electorate`. `min_electorate` and quorum stay as optional later rules (decision 18).
6. **Late votes after the deadline** and the first-close-wins race for `expired`: write `decision_closed(expired)` on first read, or only by housekeeping tick?
7. **Primitive first vs the review gate.** *Resolved (Jesse, 2026-10-24):* the `primitive` effect (#410) waits for the review gate (#391/#393) before it ships; no stopgap is needed. The decision core and relayed binding votes ship first. *Proposal for Jesse (flagged, not decided):* if an interim effect is wanted, `surface_proposal` (#408), whose apply path exists today, is the candidate (decision 18).
8. **Topic rooms.** Does the `topic` preset move to `majority` for contributions that touch a key more than one maintainer has written, or keep `single`? This ADR changes nothing about the preset.
9. **Org policy.** Should an org's policy (ADR 0031) be able to require `dash_session`-verified votes for all its rooms' `primitive`/`data_grant` decisions?
10. **Effect handlers' ownership.** `data_grant` depends on #385's grant model and #387's consent flow, `primitive` on #393; which lands first sets the delivery order.
11. **Relationship to #350.** Topic-post up/down votes feed a score and are open to readers. A decision vote is governance. They share the word and nothing else; #350's design must not reuse this event type, and its sybil answer is its own.
